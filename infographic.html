Is AI Deciding for Us?

AI can give us answers, drafts, and recommendations almost instantly. The real question is whether we are still the ones making the decision.

🗺️ I choose→📍 GPS suggests→⭐ Feeds recommend→🤖 AI decides?

01 · AI IS EVERYWHERE

It's already part of student life.

4 in 5U.S. high school and college students use AI for schoolwork.
Stanford HAI, 2026 AI Index Report (Education)

AI shows up in how we find information, finish schoolwork, organize ideas, get recommendations, write, and make everyday choices.

📚 Schoolwork🔎 Search⭐ Recommendations✍️ Writing🧩 Decisions
02 · LOOKING BACK

We've handed decisions to technology before.

GPS started as a tool to help us find our way. Then it quietly became the thing that chooses the route, turn by turn, while we just follow.

🗺️ I read the map→📍 GPS suggests a route→🚗 I just follow→🤷 I'm lost without it

Research on habitual GPS use (Dahmani & Bohbot, 2020) found that people who relied on GPS more had worse spatial memory when they had to navigate on their own. The skill we stop practicing is the skill we lose.

👇 The pattern to notice

Helpful → default → habit → dependence. Nobody forced us to give up navigating. It just became easier not to do it. GPS decides a route. AI can decide what to write, what to study, and what to believe.

03 · THE SLIDE

Convenience can quietly become dependence.

AI helps us find information, finish schoolwork, organize ideas, and get recommendations. That is genuinely useful. But there is a point where convenience turns into dependence, and it is easy to miss because every single step feels helpful.

❓ I'm stuck→⚡ Instant answer→😌 Easier next time→🫥 I stop trying first
👇 Why this matters to me as a student

Technology is already a major part of how I learn. AI can be extremely helpful when I'm struggling with a concept. But it made me ask: am I using technology as a helping tool, or letting it do the thinking for me?

04 · HUMAN AGENCY

Same tool. Two very different uses.

Human agency is our ability to make our own choices and act intentionally. AI doesn't automatically take that away. What matters is how we use it.

🧭 Support

"Explain this concept I don't understand."

My thinking is helped, and my understanding grows.

🪄 Replace

"Do this whole task for me."

I get an answer, but nothing develops in me.

07 · CONNECTION TO SULEYMAN

We can't fully control what a technology becomes.

Suleyman's "containment problem" is the difficulty of controlling a technology once it spreads. The people who built GPS wanted to give directions. They didn't plan for a generation that struggles to find its way without a screen.

Technology rarely forces us to hand over our decisions. It just makes handing them over easy.
👇 What does that mean for AI?

As AI gets more capable, it will be involved in even more of our decisions. Best case: people learn more and solve problems once out of reach. Worse case: people accept AI answers without questioning them, and critical thinking weakens. Which way it goes depends on us.

08 · TWO POSSIBLE FUTURES

Where do we end up?

✨ AI + human judgment

  • More capabilities
  • Better access to information
  • Technology supports human decisions
  • Problems once out of reach become solvable

⚠️ AI without critical thinking

  • Greater dependence
  • Less questioning of information
  • More decisions made automatically
  • Independent judgment fades from lack of practice

Both futures use the same technology. The difference is the habit we bring to it.

10 · TAKING IT WITH ME

Access to more technology is not more control.

Keeping control means choosing it, again and again. Before I accept an AI answer, I can ask:

  1. Did I try to think about it first?
  2. Do I understand why this answer works?
  3. Could I explain it without the AI?
  4. Would I make the same choice if I'd done the research myself?

AI isn't entirely good or bad. Learning to use it without losing our independence may be one of the most important challenges of our moment.
