Is AI Deciding for Us?

Artificial intelligence can now produce answers, drafts, and recommendations almost instantly, which shifts the more pressing question away from what the technology is capable of and toward whether we remain the ones making the decisions it informs.

🗺️ I choose→📍 GPS suggests→⭐ Feeds recommend→🤖 AI decides?

01 · AI IS EVERYWHERE

It's already part of student life.

4 in 5U.S. high school and college students use AI for schoolwork.
Stanford HAI, 2026 AI Index Report (Education)

AI has become woven into the ordinary routines of student life, shaping how we locate information, complete assignments, organize our thinking, and weigh everyday choices, often without our pausing to consider how much of that process we are still carrying out ourselves.

02 · LOOKING BACK

We've handed decisions to technology before.

GPS began as a tool designed to support our sense of direction, yet it gradually became the system that selects the route and issues each instruction in turn, leaving us to follow along as we slowly stop making navigational decisions of our own.

🗺️ I read the map→📍 GPS suggests a route→🚗 I just follow→🤷 I'm lost without it

Research on habitual GPS use (Dahmani & Bohbot, 2020) found that people who relied on it more heavily showed weaker spatial memory when they later had to navigate without assistance, which suggests that a skill we stop practicing is one we eventually begin to lose.

👇 The pattern to notice

Helpful → habit → default → dependence. No one required us to give up navigating for ourselves; it simply became easier to let the technology do it, and each convenience quietly made the next handoff feel more natural. GPS decides which route we take, whereas AI has the potential to influence what we write, what we study, and ultimately what we come to believe.

03 · THE SLIDE

Convenience can quietly become dependence.

The usefulness of AI is real, since it can clarify difficult material, help structure our ideas, and speed up tasks that once took considerable effort. Convenience can nevertheless turn into dependence without much notice, as each individual step feels helpful even when the overall pattern gradually moves us away from doing the thinking ourselves.

❓ I'm stuck→⚡ Instant answer→😌 Easier next time→🫥 I stop trying first
👇 Why this matters to me as a student

As a college student, I already depend on technology for a large share of how I learn, and AI can be especially helpful as I work through a concept I am struggling to understand. That experience led me to question whether I am using it as a tool that supports my thinking or allowing it to do the thinking on my behalf.

04 · HUMAN AGENCY

One tool, two very different uses.

Human agency refers to our ability to make our own choices and to act intentionally on them. AI does not automatically diminish that ability simply because it can perform certain tasks for us; what determines the outcome is the manner in which we choose to use it, and whether we remain actively involved in the process.

🧭 Support

"Explain this concept I don't understand."

The technology supports my reasoning, and my own understanding develops as a result of the exchange.

🪄 Replace

"Do this whole task for me."

As I receive a finished answer without engaging with the material, I gain a result while my own understanding stays unchanged.

07 · CONNECTION TO SULEYMAN

We can't fully control what a technology becomes.

Suleyman's "containment problem" describes the difficulty of controlling a technology once it enters society and begins to spread. The engineers who built GPS intended to provide directions, not to produce a generation that struggles to find its way without a screen, which illustrates how far the consequences of a technology can drift from the intentions behind it.

Technology rarely forces us to hand over our decisions; it simply makes handing them over easy.
👇 What does that mean for AI?

As AI grows more capable, it will likely become involved in an even wider range of the decisions we make. In the most favorable scenario, it helps people learn more and solve problems that were previously out of reach, whereas in the less favorable one, people grow accustomed to accepting AI-generated answers without questioning them, and critical thinking weakens. Which direction we move in depends largely on the habits we build now.

08 · TWO POSSIBLE FUTURES

Where do we end up?

✨ AI + human judgment

  • More capabilities
  • Better access to information
  • Technology supports human decisions
  • Problems once out of reach become solvable

⚠️ AI without critical thinking

  • Greater dependence
  • Less questioning of information
  • More decisions made automatically
  • Independent judgment fades from lack of practice

Both futures rely on exactly the same technology, so the difference between them lies less in how advanced the systems become and more in the habits of questioning, verification, and independent judgment that we bring to them.

10 · TAKING IT WITH ME

Access to more technology is not more control.

Maintaining control over our decisions is a practice that must be renewed each time we turn to a tool for help, rather than a single choice made once. Before accepting an AI-generated answer, it is worth asking:

  1. Did I try to think about it first?
  2. Do I understand why this answer works?
  3. Could I explain it without the AI?
  4. Would I make the same choice if I'd done the research myself?

Access to more technology does not automatically translate into more control over our lives, and the future of AI will depend on how advanced the systems become as much as on how deliberately we choose to interact with them. Learning to use AI without losing our independence of thought may prove to be one of the most important challenges of this period.
