Is AI Helping Us Think—or Thinking for Us?

AI is becoming part of everyday life. The important question is not only what it can do, but how we choose to use it.

AI is already part of student life: Stanford HAI's 2026 AI Index reports that four out of five U.S. high school and college students use AI for schoolwork.

1AI is everywhere

AI is increasingly appearing in education, work, search, recommendations, writing, creativity, and everyday decision-making.

SchoolWork SearchRecommendations WritingDecisions

2AI can support our thinking

When used intentionally, AI can help explain difficult ideas, organize information, brainstorm, and show different perspectives.

Question→ AI helps→ I understand→ I decide

3But AI can also replace our thinking

If we automatically accept AI answers, we may practice less independent problem-solving and critical evaluation.

Question→ AI answers→ I accept→ I don't question

4Try the question yourself

How much of our thinking are we willing to give to technology?

Click an option to see the difference between using AI as a tool and letting it replace human judgment.

AI as a tool: The technology supports your process. You evaluate the information, add your own reasoning, and make the final decision.
AI as a decision-maker: The technology increasingly determines the answer while the human simply accepts it. This can reduce opportunities to question, learn, and exercise independent judgment.

Human agency means having the ability to make our own choices and act intentionally. NIST emphasizes that human roles and responsibilities in AI decision-making and oversight should be clearly defined.

5The future: two possible paths

AI + Human Judgment

  • More capabilities
  • Better access to information
  • Technology supports human decisions

AI Without Critical Thinking

  • Greater dependence
  • Less questioning of information
  • More decisions made automatically
